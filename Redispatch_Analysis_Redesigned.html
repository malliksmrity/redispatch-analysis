Redispatch Analysis · SMARD & Netztransparenz, Jul 2022 – Apr 2026

Grid congestion in Germany: the cost of redispatch, and the case for investment

Business question: Is Germany's redispatch cost rising, what is driving it, and should the committee fund transmission investment on that basis?

Short answer: cost per event has fallen sharply since the 2023 peak, but the underlying congestion volume hasn't — so the case for investment rests on frequency, not on a runaway bill.

46 months of SMARD cost & volume data · 51,443 individual redispatch measures from Netztransparenz · hourly generation data for the wind comparison.

Chart 1 · Cost trend

Monthly redispatch cost has fallen 61% since its January 2023 peak

Monthly spend (bars) against its trailing 12-month average (line) — the average is what separates a real trend from one noisy month.

Monthly cost 12-month average

Cost peaked at €349m in January 2023 and sits at €136m in April 2026 — a real, sustained decline the 12-month line confirms is not just a quiet month. It ticks up slightly at the very end; too soon to call that a reversal. This 61% figure is measured against the single most expensive month on record, so it describes the scale of recovery from an unusual peak, not a smooth year-over-year decline.

Chart 2 · Wind vs. redispatch volume

Wind generation and redispatch volume move together — until they don't

Both series indexed to their starting month (Mar 2025 = 100) so they share one axis instead of two independent scales, which can be stretched to imply a correlation that isn't there.

Redispatch volume Wind generation

The two series track closely from March through November 2025. Then they split: wind stays elevated from December through February while redispatch volume drops sharply in February before rebounding. Wind explains part of the swing, but clearly not all of it — an investment case built solely on wind growth would be overreaching.

Only 14 months of hourly generation data overlap with the monthly redispatch series, so the December–February divergence should be read as one observed episode, not yet a confirmed seasonal pattern.

Chart 3 · Unit cost by year

Every euro of redispatch buys more: unit cost has fallen every year since 2022

Cost per megawatt-hour of redispatched energy, by year. Each dot is one month; the box spans the middle 50% of months, the line inside it is the median.

The median cost per MWh drops every year — from about €129 in 2022 to roughly €68 in 2025 — and the boxes narrow too, so it isn't just cheaper, it's steadier. That backs Chart 1 from a different angle: it's not that redispatch happens less, each instance genuinely costs less to resolve than it used to.

2022 (6 months) and 2026 (4 months) are partial years with far fewer observations than 2023–2025, so their boxes reflect a noisier sample. Even allowing for that, no year has cost more per MWh than the one before it.

Chart 4 · Grid operator comparison

TenneT DE carries nearly half of all redispatch energy nationally

Total energy redispatched by the instructing transmission system operator, summed across all 51,443 recorded measures.

TenneT DE handles about 27,100 GWh — roughly double Amprion and almost four times TransnetBW, the smallest of the four. If transmission investment is the fix that gets funded, TenneT's stretch of the grid is the obvious place to start; this ranking held consistently across the full period, not just in one unusual month.

Chart 5 · Volume composition

Total redispatch volume shows no downward trend — only the cost of it does

Monthly redispatched energy, split between conventional and renewable plants. The total swings between roughly 700 and 3,500 GWh with no sustained decline.

Conventional Renewable

Congestion is just as frequent as it was in 2022 — only cheaper to resolve each time. This is arguably the single most important finding here: taken alone, the falling cost in Chart 1 would suggest congestion is easing, but the volume driving it hasn't moved.

Chart 6 · Reduction vs. increase balance

Reductions are growing while increases shrink — a quietly shifting burden

Average monthly volume, comparing the earliest full period (2022–23) against the most recent (2025–26).

Reductions — which typically fall on renewable plants told to curtail output — rose from about 1,215 to 1,300 GWh a month. Increases, mostly conventional plants ramping up, fell more sharply, from about 810 to 635 GWh a month. A growing share of reductions means renewable generators are quietly absorbing more of this burden, even as the headline cost numbers look better.

Recommendation

Proceed with transmission investment, prioritised in TenneT DE's corridor. The spending case should rest on the fact that congestion volume hasn't fallen in four years (Chart 5), not on the cost trend — which is a story about efficiency gains, not reduced need (Charts 1 & 3). Because the recent cost uptick is only a few months old and wind explains only part of the volume swings (Chart 2), the committee should treat this as a directional case for grid capacity, and revisit sizing once another 6–12 months of data confirm whether the late-period tick in cost holds.

  • Cost per event is down 61% from peak and unit cost has fallen every year since 2022 — redispatch is being resolved more efficiently.
  • Underlying volume has not trended down at all, swinging 700–3,500 GWh/month throughout — the root cause (congestion) is unchanged.
  • TenneT DE's network carries ~47% of all redispatch energy nationally — the highest-leverage location for investment.
  • Renewable generators are absorbing a growing share of curtailment (reductions), a distributional effect worth flagging alongside the cost case.
