Germany’s grid-congestion bill is falling, but the amount of power moved around is not

When the grid is overloaded, operators tell some power plants to produce less and others more. This is called redispatch. Each unit now costs about 68 euros per MWh, down from about 129 in 2022. The monthly volume has no clear downward trend.

Data: SMARD (Bundesnetzagentur) and Netztransparenz, July 2022 to April 2026.

What the numbers show

Are these trends real, or just noise?

Each month is compared with the same month a year earlier, so winter peaks do not distort the result. With bootstrap intervals (a 95 percent range from resampling):

How it works

  1. Take monthly cost and volume from SMARD and the list of individual measures from Netztransparenz.
  2. Divide cost by volume to get a cost per MWh, and sum the measures by grid operator.
  3. Compare with wind generation for the months where both exist (13 months): the two move together (correlation 0.75), but with so few months the plausible range is wide (0.31 to 0.96).

Limits

  • 46 months only, and the first and last years are partial.
  • Costs include compensation payments and are what the data source reports. They are not checked against operator accounts.
  • The recommendation in the original report (invest in transmission, starting with TenneT’s area) is a reading of these charts, not a cost-benefit study.
  • Wind and redispatch are linked in this data, but wind alone does not explain the swings.
